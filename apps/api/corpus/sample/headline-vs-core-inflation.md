# Headline vs core inflation

Source: Statistics South Africa, Statistical Release P0141 (Consumer Price Index), July 2026.
Full release: https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0141/P0141July2026.pdf

## Headline inflation

Headline inflation is the annual change in the **all items** CPI: the full basket of goods and
services bought by urban households. It is the number quoted in news reports and the target
referenced by monetary policy. In July 2026 headline inflation was **4,3%**, down from 5,0% in
June 2026.

## Core inflation

Core inflation tries to show the underlying trend by removing items whose prices jump around
for temporary reasons. Stats SA publishes two core measures:

1. **CPI excluding food and non-alcoholic beverages, fuel and energy** — the most commonly
   cited core measure. Food and fuel are removed because weather, harvests and global oil
   prices can move them sharply and then reverse.
2. **Trimmed mean** — an analytical series that drops the items with the most extreme monthly
   price changes in each direction, so one-off shocks do not distort the average.

In July 2026:

| Measure                                  | Annual (y/y) |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| Headline CPI (all items)                 | 4,3%         |
| CPI excluding food, NAB, fuel and energy | 4,2%         |
| Trimmed mean                             | 3,1%         |

## Why the gap matters

- When headline inflation is **above** core, the extra pressure usually comes from food or
  fuel and may fade as those shocks unwind. Example: May 2026 headline was 4,5% while the core
  measure was 3,8% — fuel price increases drove most of the gap.
- When headline is **below** core, as in July 2026 (4,3% vs a 3,4% trimmed mean a month
  earlier), food and fuel are pulling the average down while underlying price pressure remains.
- Core is not a promise that prices will fall. It measures whether inflation is becoming more
  broad-based across the basket.

## Related series in the same release

- **CPI for goods vs services**: goods inflation was 3,4% in July 2026, while services
  inflation was 5,0% — services have run above goods for most of 2026.
- **Administered prices**: prices set by government or regulators (electricity, water, fuel
  levies, some transport). They rose 10,9% in July 2026, well above the headline rate.
- **CPI excluding administered prices**: 3,3% in July 2026, showing that much of the remaining
  pressure sits in regulated prices.
